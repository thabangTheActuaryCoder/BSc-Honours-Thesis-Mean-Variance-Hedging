# Mean-Variance Hedging with Neural Networks using Vector AutoRegression Models as the Underlying Sample Path Generator

BSc Honours thesis submitted for the degree **Bachelor of Science with Honours (Statistics & Data Science)** at the University of Cape Town, Department of Statistical Sciences.

**Authors:** Baloyi Thabang Bongani Junior, Ditiro Letsoalo
**Supervisors:** Yuri Robbertze, Jake Stangroom
**Date:** November 2024

## Overview

This thesis develops a discrete-time mean-variance hedging framework that uses feedforward neural networks trained on simulated asset price paths generated by a Vector AutoRegression (VAR) model. The approach is compared against the traditional Black-Scholes delta hedging strategy.

The core problem: in incomplete markets, perfect replication of derivative payoffs is unattainable. The objective is to find a self-financing trading strategy that minimises the expected squared replication error between the terminal portfolio value and a spread option payoff on two correlated assets (Chevron and ExxonMobil).

## Approach

1. **VAR model fitting** -- A VAR(11) model is fitted to the stationary log return series of CVX and XOM, capturing temporal correlations and cross-dependencies between the two stocks.

2. **Sample path generation** -- The fitted VAR model generates realistic simulated price paths that reflect the multivariate time series dynamics of the underlying assets.

3. **Neural network training** -- A feedforward neural network with two hidden layers of 25 neurons each (sigmoid activation, Adam optimiser, learning rate 0.01) is trained to learn the optimal hedging strategy by minimising the expected squared hedging error.

4. **Comparison with Black-Scholes** -- The neural network hedging strategy is evaluated against the Black-Scholes delta hedge on both simulated (5 000 paths) and real market data.

## Key Results

| Strategy | Mean PNL | Variance of PNL |
|---|---|---|
| Black-Scholes | 0.0149 | 0.00002 |
| Neural Network | 0.0159 | 0.0002 |

- The Black-Scholes hedge achieves lower PNL variance, indicating more stable and consistent hedging performance on VAR-simulated data.
- The neural network produces a slightly higher mean PNL but with greater variance, suggesting it adapts to market dynamics differently but with less consistency.
- On real market data, the neural network adapts more smoothly to mid-path fluctuations while Black-Scholes shows more sensitivity to its own model assumptions.
- The results suggest neural networks have potential to outperform traditional methods, particularly with more sophisticated architectures (RNNs, LSTMs) and additional computational resources.

## Methods and Tools

- **Econometrics:** Vector AutoRegression (VAR), ADF and KPSS stationarity tests, lag order selection
- **Pricing model:** Black-Scholes for European spread options
- **Neural network:** Feedforward architecture (2 hidden layers, 25 neurons each), grid search hyperparameter tuning
- **Data:** Chevron (CVX) and ExxonMobil (XOM) historical closing prices
- **Language:** Python
